Section 1.8: Solving Absolute-Value Equations and Inequalities

Metric distance on the real number line, compound conjunctions and disjunctions, and tolerance modeling.

Methodological Note. Enter exact simplified answers—fractions such as -8/3, compound inequalities such as -9 < x < 6, or comma-separated pairs—directly on the ruled lines. Each exercise allows two attempts before revealing the complete derivation and number-line representation in the margin.

Geometrically, the expression $|x - a|$ measures the undirected Euclidean distance between a variable coordinate $x$ and a fixed center $a$ on the real number line. For any positive radius $c > 0$, comparing $|x - a|$ against $c$ partitions the real line into three canonical solution geometries:

0 Isolate Before Splitting. Always isolate the absolute-value bars on one side of the equation or inequality before rewriting into two linear branches. Furthermore, because $|u| \ge 0$ for every real $u$, any inequality of the form $|u| < c$ with $c \le 0$ has no real solution.

7 Symmetric Confidence Interval. A sample statistic $\hat{p} = 68\%$ with margin of error $E = 3\%$ defines the inequality $|p - 68| \le 3$, placing the true population percent between $\hat{p} - E$ and $\hat{p} + E$.
